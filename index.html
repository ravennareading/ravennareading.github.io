Learn to read,
together.

Inky is a young raven who loves shiny pebbles, and she wants to read. On a walk through Seattle's Ravenna neighborhood, your child helps her collect a sound pebble for every letter sound, push sounds together into words, and earn eight little books they can really read. It is built for two: a child who isn't reading yet, and a grown-up beside them.

Together is the point

It starts with a high five.

Children learn to read fastest beside someone who loves them. So Ravenna Reading doesn't try to replace you. It hands you the script.

  • Every lesson opens with a big hand and a little hand on the screen at the same time.
  • Each card has a short line for you to say, a tip on how to help, and two buttons: Got it and Help.
  • You are the judge. There is no microphone: a person hears a four-year-old far better than an app can.
  • Help is never wrong. A sound that needed help just comes back sooner in the warm-ups.

A lesson

Sounds first.
Then push them together.

A lesson is about ten minutes. It warms up with pebbles your child already has, adds one new sound, and then puts the sounds to work.

  • The new pebble: the letter, its picture, and how to say it. We teach the sound (mmm), not the letter's name.
  • Ears, finger, eyes: tap the pictures that start with the sound, finger-paint the letter, then find every pebble that has it.
  • Blend: slide a finger under a word. Each letter plays its sound as the finger passes. Then say it fast.
  • Read: a whole sentence, one word at a time.

Real reading

No guessing from the picture.

A child who guesses "sun" from a picture of a sun hasn't read anything. So in Ravenna Reading the picture stays hidden until the word has been read, and pops up as the reward.

  • Every word your child is asked to read uses only the sounds they have been taught. The game checks this for every word, sentence and book page.
  • A few common words that don't follow the rules yet (the, said, was) are nest words. Inky keeps them in her nest, and they are learned whole.
  • Lower-case letters first, in a typeface drawn for new readers.

The books

"I read it all by myself!"

Each of the eight places ends with a little book. You read the title; your child reads the pages. Then the book goes on their shelf, to read again to a grandparent, a sibling or a stuffed animal.

  • Eight original stories, from Sam Sat (four sounds) to The Best Trip (all of them).
  • Two questions to ask after every book, because talking about a story matters as much as reading its words.
  • On the shelf, tap any word to hear it, or have a page read aloud.

On their own

Practice while you make dinner.

New sounds always wait for a grown-up. But three corners of the map only use what your child has already learned, so they are fine to visit alone.

  • Play: four games. Hear a sound and pick its pebble, find the first sound of a picture, read a word and tap its picture, or build a word from pebbles.
  • Books: the shelf of every book earned so far.
  • Pebbles: Inky's collection. Tap a pebble to hear its sound.
  • No timers and no losing. After two misses the answer glows.

Eight places, 44 little lessons

The walk follows real public places in Ravenna: the boulevard, Cowen Park, and the ravine, creek and playfield of Ravenna Park. The houses and the bookshop are made up.

  1. The Front Porchm, a, t, s, and the first words: am, at, mat, sat. Book: Sam Sat.
  2. Ravenna Boulevardp, i, n, d, under the big old trees, and two sounds side by side at the end (sand). Book: Pip the Ant.
  3. Cowen Parko, c, g, h, by the swings and the sand. Book: To the Top.
  4. The Ravine Trailb, u, r, f, down under the tall trees. Book: Mud Pup.
  5. Ravenna Creekl, e, k, w, by the water, and two sounds side by side at the start (frog). Book: Wet Legs.
  6. The High Bridgej, v, y, z, x, qu, up among the treetops. Book: Jam Buns.
  7. The Playfieldsh, ch, th, ck: two letters, one sound. Book: Chip the Duck.
  8. The Book Nookll, ss, ff, zz, ng, side-by-side sounds at both ends (stamp), and long words read in parts (picnic). Book: The Best Trip.

For grown-ups

Ravenna Reading was made by a parent in Seattle, as the youngest sister of Maple Leaf Math and Licton Springs Language. Here's what you can expect.

🧑‍🏫 No training needed

You don't need to know how reading is taught. Each card tells you what to say, how to make the sound with your mouth, and what to do if your child is stuck.

👄 Clean sounds

The grown-ups' page shows how to say all 35 sounds: which can be stretched (mmm, sss) and which are quick (t, b), with no "uh" on the end.

🌱 Something for later

Every lesson ends with one idea away from the screen: a sound hunt, a letter drawn in flour, or simply a picture book read aloud at bedtime.

📈 See how it's going

How many times you've read together and on how many days, which sounds are firm and which are shaky, and every book on the shelf.

⏩ Start where they are

If your child already knows the first sounds, open a later place from the grown-ups' page. Any lesson can be played again, as often as you like.

⏳ No pressure

No timers, no streaks to lose, no scores, nothing marked wrong. A miss dims a pebble and plays a soft low note.

🔒 Private by design

No ads, accounts, analytics, tracking or microphone, and the app never goes online. Privacy policy

📚 Keep reading aloud

This game teaches how letters turn into words. The love of stories still comes from you reading real books to them, every day.
