Support

Found a bug, heard a sound that seems off, or have an idea? We'd love to hear from you.

Get in touch

Email bugjugapp@gmail.com. It helps to tell us your device, the place and lesson (for example "Cowen Park, the g lesson"), and what you expected to happen. If you use GitHub, you can also open an issue.

Common questions

Is it out yet?

Not yet. Ravenna Reading is still being finished. The home page will have the store links when it is ready.

What ages is it for?

Children of about 3 to 6 who are not reading yet, together with a grown-up. A child who knows some letter sounds already can start at a later place.

Can my child play it alone?

Partly. Lessons, where new sounds are taught, need a grown-up: they start with a high five and each card has a part for you. Play, Books and Pebbles on the map only use what your child has already learned, so those are fine alone.

I only have one hand free. How do I do the high five?

Hold the grown-up hand for a moment and the lesson starts.

Why is there no microphone?

Because you are better at it. A person beside a child can hear whether a small voice said "mmm", can tell a guess from a near miss, and can smile. You tap Got it when they have it, or Help to hear the sound and try again. Help is never wrong; that sound just comes back sooner in warm-ups.

Why sounds and not letter names?

Words are made of sounds. "Em, ay, tee" doesn't turn into "mat", but "mmm, aaa, t" does. Letter names can come later. Keep the sounds clean: mmm and sss can be stretched; t and b are quick, with no "uh" on the end. The grown-ups' page has a Sounds tab that shows how to say every one.

My child is stuck on blending.

That is the hardest step, and it is normal for it to take a while. Slide your own finger under the word and stretch the sounds into each other without stopping, then let them try. Tap Help to hear each sound and then the word. Repeat lessons as often as you like, and try saying words slowly away from the screen ("can you pass the mmm-ugg?").

What are nest words?

A few very common words, like the, said and was, don't follow the rules a new reader knows. Inky keeps them in her nest and they are learned whole, one at a time: sixteen in all.

My child already knows some letters. Do we have to start at the beginning?

No. On the grown-ups' page, open Progress and choose Start further along. Everything before the place you pick counts as done.

How do I get to the grown-ups' page?

Tap Grown-ups on the title screen or the map, and answer a quick sum.

How long is a lesson, and how often should we play?

About ten minutes. One lesson on most days works better than a long sitting once a week. Stop while it is still fun.

Does it need the internet?

No. Ravenna Reading works fully offline, even in airplane mode.

Will it teach my child everything about reading?

No. It teaches the first big step: the common sound of each letter, and how to blend sounds into short words, sentences and little books. Long vowels and trickier spellings come after that. And nothing replaces reading real books aloud to your child every day.

Terms

Ravenna Reading is a game for learning and practicing early reading. It is not a test, a diagnosis or a substitute for a teacher. If you are worried about your child's speech, hearing or reading, talk to their doctor or teacher.

The walk in the game is inspired by real public places in Seattle's Ravenna neighborhood: Ravenna Boulevard, Cowen Park, and the ravine, creek and playfield of Ravenna Park. The map is a picture, not a street map. The characters, the house with the front porch and the bookshop are made up, and any resemblance to real people, homes or businesses is a coincidence.
